Harborline Mutual Insurance: Q3 2026 Operations Review

Requests received July 1 to September 25, 2026. Partial period: the quarter ends September 30, so both comparisons are cut to the same number of days (April 1 to June 26, 2026 and July 1 to September 25, 2025).

Requests rose 43.6% on last year, and on-time delivery fell to 48.7% against your contracted clocks. The drop came in two steps: in February, when the new 5-day and 3-day clocks took effect, and in July, when average turnaround rose from 3.86 to 5.17 business days, a month before August's volume peak.

What drove it

  1. The February clock change. Turnaround held steady (3.93 days in January, 3.62 in February), but on-time delivery moved from 95.5% to 80.2% because the clocks shortened. Subpoenas carried most of it: from February to June they averaged 4.16 days against a 3-day clock, and 29.1% were on time, while other requests stayed at 94.9%.
  2. The July slowdown. Average turnaround went from 3.86 days in June to 5.17 in July and rose across all four request types. July volume (88) was level with June (86), so the slowdown came before the volume increase, not because of it. August then brought 140 requests, the busiest month in the last 18.
  3. Subpoenas are furthest behind. 5.2% of the 77 subpoenas completed this quarter met the 3-day clock (average 5.68 days). On the 5-day clock: authorizations 63.6%, letter requests 67.3%, court orders 72.2%.
  4. Volume growth was broad. Requests were up 92 on last year, and 22 of 33 requestors sent more. The five fastest-growing account for 41.7% of the increase: RecordPoint Retrieval (+13) and Keystone Record Retrieval (+10) lead. By type, plaintiff attorneys (+43) and record retrieval companies (+35) make up 84.7% of the growth.
